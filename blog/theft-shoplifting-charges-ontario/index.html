Theft & shoplifting charges: a first-offence guide

Most shoplifting cases in Ontario are charged as theft under $5,000 under section 334 of the Criminal Code. For a first-time accused in Mississauga or Brampton, the charge does not always end in a criminal record: depending on the facts, the Crown can offer diversion, which leads to the charge being withdrawn, and a court can grant a discharge instead of a conviction.

Key points

  • Theft under $5,000 is the usual charge for shoplifting
  • Diversion can lead to the charge being withdrawn
  • A discharge is not a conviction, and its record is removed after one or three years
  • A store’s letter demanding money is separate from the criminal case

What is the difference between theft under and theft over $5,000?

Section 334 of the Criminal Code sets the penalties by value. Theft under $5,000 can be prosecuted summarily or by indictment, with a maximum of two years in prison on indictment. Theft over $5,000 is an indictable offence with a maximum of ten years. Related charges include possession of property obtained by crime (section 354) and, where deception is involved, fraud. See our guide to fraud charges.

What happens after a shoplifting arrest?

Most people are released at the store or the police station with paperwork that sets two dates: one for fingerprints and photographs, and a first appearance at the Ontario Court of Justice in Brampton. Attend both. Missing either is a separate offence. At the first appearance, the Crown provides disclosure, the evidence it has, such as store video and the loss-prevention report.

What is diversion, and can I get it?

Diversion lets the Crown deal with a less serious charge outside the normal court process. In Ontario, a first-time accused facing a minor theft may be offered a program where they accept responsibility and complete a requirement, such as community service, a charitable donation, counselling or paying back the loss. Once it is completed, the charge is withdrawn and there is no finding of guilt. Diversion is at the Crown’s discretion, and a lawyer can often help by raising it early with the right background information.

What is a discharge, and is it a criminal record?

If a case is not diverted, a court can grant a discharge under section 730 instead of a conviction. An absolute discharge has no conditions; a conditional discharge comes with probation. Under the Criminal Records Act, the record of an absolute discharge is removed from the national police database after one year, and a conditional discharge after three years. A conviction, by contrast, stays on record until a record suspension is granted.

The store sent me a letter asking for money. Do I have to pay?

Some retailers send a “civil demand” letter asking for a payment to cover their costs. That letter is separate from the criminal charge: paying it does not end the criminal case, and not paying it does not make the charge worse. Get advice on your situation before you respond.

Can my fingerprints and photos be destroyed?

If the charge is withdrawn, stayed or ends in an acquittal, you can usually ask the police service that took your fingerprints and photographs to destroy them. Each service has its own application process, and it is worth doing so the records do not appear in future checks.

For people who are not Canadian citizens, even a minor theft can raise immigration questions. Maks Law Firm’s criminal defence lawyers in Mississauga can advise on both.

FAQ

Common questions

General information about Ontario law, not legal advice for your situation.

Will I go to jail for a first shoplifting charge?

Custody is uncommon for a first, low-value theft, and diversion or a discharge is often possible. But every case depends on its facts, including the value, the circumstances and any record, so get advice before your first court date.

Do I need a lawyer for a first offence?

It is strongly worth speaking to one. A lawyer can review the disclosure, raise diversion with the Crown and make sure the outcome protects your record, employment and travel plans.

Will a withdrawn charge show up on a background check?

A withdrawn charge is not a conviction, but police records can remain until they are destroyed. Some vulnerable sector checks can show non-conviction information, which is why applying to destroy fingerprints and records matters.

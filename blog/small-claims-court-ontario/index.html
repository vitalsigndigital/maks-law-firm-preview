Small Claims Court in Ontario: claims up to $50,000

Ontario’s Small Claims Court hears money claims of up to $50,000, excluding interest and costs, a limit in place since October 1, 2025. Disputes from Mississauga are usually filed in the Small Claims Court in Brampton, which serves Peel Region. People can represent themselves or be represented by a lawyer or a licensed paralegal.

Key points

  • The limit is $50,000, excluding interest and costs
  • A defendant generally has 20 days to file a defence after being served
  • Every defended case goes to a settlement conference before trial
  • Costs for representation are usually capped at 15% of the amount claimed

What kinds of claims go to Small Claims Court?

Small Claims Court deals with claims for money, or for the return of personal property, worth up to $50,000. Common examples are unpaid invoices and loans, contract disputes, property damage, deposits that were not returned and disputes with contractors. Some matters belong elsewhere: residential tenancy disputes go to the Landlord and Tenant Board, and family matters go to family court.

How do I start a claim?

  1. Send a demand letter. It is not required, but many disputes settle at this stage, and it shows the court you tried.
  2. File a Plaintiff’s Claim (Form 7A) online or at the court office, in the area where the events happened or where the defendant lives or does business.
  3. Serve the defendant with the claim within six months of filing, following the rules on how service must be done.

Check the deadline first: under Ontario’s Limitations Act, 2002, most claims must be started within two years of when you discovered the problem.

I have been served with a claim. What now?

A defendant generally has 20 days after being served to file a Defence. If you do nothing, the plaintiff can have you noted in default and get judgment without hearing your side. A Defence can dispute all or part of the claim, propose a payment plan, or include a claim of your own against the plaintiff or someone else.

What happens at the settlement conference?

Every defended case goes to a settlement conference with a judge or deputy judge before any trial. The parties exchange their documents and witness lists, the judge helps narrow the issues and gives a frank view of the case, and many claims settle there. If they do not, the case is set down for trial.

Who pays the legal costs?

The successful party usually recovers its court fees and some expenses. Under section 29 of the Courts of Justice Act, an award for a lawyer’s or paralegal’s representation is generally capped at 15% of the amount claimed, unless the court decides to penalize a party for unreasonable behaviour. Settlement offers made in writing can also affect costs.

Do I need a lawyer or a paralegal?

You can represent yourself, and many people do. Representation helps most with getting the evidence in order, valuing the claim, negotiating, and presenting the case at trial. Licensed paralegals are authorized by the Law Society of Ontario to appear in Small Claims Court. FAQ

Common questions

General information about Ontario law, not legal advice for your situation.

What if my claim is worth more than $50,000?

You can sue in the Superior Court of Justice, where the Simplified Procedure covers most claims up to $200,000, or you can choose to give up the amount above $50,000 to stay in Small Claims Court.

How long does a Small Claims case take?

It depends on the court’s schedule and whether the case settles. Many cases resolve at or before the settlement conference; a case that goes to trial can take a year or more.

Can I claim interest on the money owed?

Yes. You can claim interest at the rate agreed in your contract, or otherwise at the pre-judgment interest rate under the Courts of Justice Act.

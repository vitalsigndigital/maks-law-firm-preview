Family & divorce lawyers in Mississauga

Maks Law Firm’s family lawyers in Mississauga help clients through separation and divorce — parenting arrangements, child and spousal support, division of property and separation agreements. Where it suits the family, we work toward negotiated or mediated settlements; where it doesn’t, we represent clients in Ontario family court.

Do I qualify for a divorce in Ontario?

Divorce in Canada is governed by the federal Divorce Act. The most common ground is that the spouses have lived separate and apart for at least one year. You can start the application before the year is up, and spouses can sometimes be “separated” while still living under the same roof. Adultery and cruelty are also grounds, but are rarely relied on.

How are parenting arrangements decided?

Since March 1, 2021, Canadian and Ontario family law use the terms decision-making responsibility and parenting time instead of “custody” and “access”. Every decision is made according to the best interests of the child — including the child’s needs, relationships, safety and views.

How are support and property worked out?

Child support is generally set by the Federal Child Support Guidelines, based on the paying parent’s income and the number of children. Spousal support depends on entitlement, and its amount and duration are usually guided by the Spousal Support Advisory Guidelines. Married spouses in Ontario share the growth in their wealth during the marriage through an equalization of net family property; common-law partners do not have the same automatic right, which makes advice on their property claims especially important.

FAQ

Family Law questions

General information about Ontario law, not legal advice for your situation.

Can I just separate without getting divorced?

Yes. Many couples separate and resolve parenting, support and property in a separation agreement without divorcing. A divorce is only needed if you want to legally end the marriage — for example, to remarry.

How long does a divorce take in Ontario?

An uncontested divorce, where everything else is settled, typically moves faster than one where parenting, support or property are still in dispute. Timelines also depend on the court’s schedule; we will give you a realistic estimate for your situation.

Who gets the house when we separate?

For married spouses, both have an equal right to possess the matrimonial home until there is an agreement or court order, regardless of whose name is on title. Its value is then dealt with as part of equalization. Common-law partners’ rights depend on ownership and other claims.

How is child support calculated?

In most cases from the tables in the Federal Child Support Guidelines, using the paying parent’s annual income and the number of children. Special or extraordinary expenses — such as childcare or medical costs — are usually shared in proportion to income.

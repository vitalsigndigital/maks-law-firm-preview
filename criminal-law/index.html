Criminal defence lawyers in Mississauga

Maks Law Firm defends people charged with criminal and provincial offences in Mississauga, Brampton and across Ontario. Our lawyers advise from the first contact with police through bail, disclosure, resolution discussions and trial — on assault, theft and property offences, impaired and other driving offences, and more serious charges.

What should I do if I have been arrested or charged?

Ask to speak to a lawyer and then exercise your right to remain silent. Section 10(b) of the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms gives anyone arrested or detained the right to retain and instruct counsel without delay, and to be told of that right. Be polite, provide identification where required, and do not discuss the allegations with police, friends or on social media until you have advice.

What criminal charges do we defend?

  • Assault and offences of violence
  • Theft, robbery, fraud and other property offences
  • Impaired driving and other Criminal Code driving offences
  • Highway Traffic Act and other provincial offences
  • Sexual offences
  • Weapons and drug offences
  • Breach of bail or probation conditions
  • More serious charges, including homicide

Our licensed paralegal can also represent clients in many provincial offence matters, such as Highway Traffic Act tickets, within the scope the Law Society of Ontario allows.

What does a criminal lawyer do for me?

  • Attends the police station and speaks with you while you are in custody
  • Prepares and runs your bail hearing, including working with sureties
  • Obtains and reviews the Crown’s disclosure — the evidence against you
  • Investigates, gathers records and interviews witnesses
  • Brings Charter applications and other pre-trial motions
  • Negotiates with the Crown and, where appropriate, defends you at trial

What is disclosure, and why does it matter?

Since the Supreme Court of Canada’s decision in R. v. Stinchcombe (1991), the Crown must disclose all relevant information in its possession to the defence. Reviewing that disclosure carefully is the foundation of every defence: it shows how strong the case really is, and where the investigation may have gone wrong.

FAQ

Criminal Law questions

General information about Ontario law, not legal advice for your situation.

Should I talk to the police without a lawyer?

Generally, no. You have the right to remain silent and the right to speak to a lawyer without delay after arrest or detention. Anything you say can be used as evidence. Get advice first.

What happens at a bail hearing?

A justice decides whether you can be released while the case is ongoing, and on what conditions. The Crown may consent to release or ask for detention; the defence may propose a release plan, sometimes with a surety — a person who supervises you and may pledge money.

Will I end up with a criminal record?

Not necessarily. Depending on the charge and the evidence, outcomes can include withdrawal of charges, diversion, a peace bond, an acquittal or a discharge. A lawyer can tell you which outcomes are realistic once disclosure has been reviewed.

Can I share the details of my case with a lawyer confidentially?

Yes. Communications with your lawyer for the purpose of legal advice are protected by solicitor–client privilege, and Ontario lawyers have a professional duty of confidentiality. Please don’t send those details through a website form — tell us in person or by phone.
